Touch is a fundamentally human gesture that reflects not just our capacity for compassion and care, but also of resistance andaggression. Touch is an important device in the performing arts – musicians have a certain touch on their instruments, and dancersin contact improvisation use body collisions in their choreography.

Body and touch are products of a hybrid construction: even in early modern times, the sense of time through feeling the pulsecreated rhythmic communication in polyphonic music (Guido 2017, Frobenius 1972-2006, 2). In contrast to modern conducting ormechanical methods of timekeeping, these points of contact were places of participation, of shared time, of negotiating power(Forscher Weiss 2000, 2005). The role of costumes and garments in early modern opera and theatre corresponded to interactionfound in polyphonic music (Bolzoni 2001, 163, Jeßulat 2021a).

In the contemporary everyday, touch has been appropriated by the technologies of late Western capitalism, making human contactnormative and reductive. Smartphones use touchscreens and biometrics to identify and track us. Digital touch, we argue, is brittleand lacks subtlety and sociality. How can we recover the sensuous, expressive dimension of touch? We will experiment withtechnologies of physiological sensing and conductive thread to create new instruments and musical performance systems. Our aimis to understand what changes when wearable touch technologies merge with artificially intelligent communication systems,drawing from knowledge across philosophy, embodied cognition, interactive music and fashion studies.


## Research Objectives

Our objectives are to: 
- Explore group behaviour in music, and build up a hermeneutics of ‘feel.’ 
- Develop technologies informed by theoretical understandings of touch. 
- Propose transferable methodologies for assessing AI supported touch technologies facilitating interaction between people, and between people and computational systems. 
- Re-enact and extend musical practices with interactive touch systems to understand existing cultural practices and allowing new practices to emerge.
